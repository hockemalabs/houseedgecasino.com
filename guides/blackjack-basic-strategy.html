Blackjack basic strategy: the complete chart, explained

Basic strategy is the mathematically best play for every hand you can be dealt against every dealer up card. Learn it and the house edge drops to about half a percent. Here is the full chart for the rules House Edge Casino uses, with the reasoning behind each decision.

The rules this chart assumes

Basic strategy depends on the rules. This chart is for the most common Las Vegas setup, which is also the rule set in House Edge Casino's blackjack table:

  • Six decks
  • Dealer hits soft 17
  • Blackjack pays 3 to 2
  • Double on any two cards, including after a split
  • Split any pair of 10-value cards; split aces receive one card each
  • Dealer peeks for blackjack

Why: With 12 through 16 you are in the danger zone: hitting risks a bust, standing hopes the dealer busts. Against a dealer 2 through 6 the dealer busts often enough that standing wins more. Against 7 or higher the dealer usually makes a hand, so you must hit and take your chances. The one exception, hitting 12 against a 2 or 3, is because those cards bust the dealer less often than people assume.

Doubling 10 and 11 is the highest expected-value play in the game. You have a strong chance of landing a 20 or 21 with one card, and you want as much money out as possible when the odds favor you.

Why: A soft hand cannot bust on the next card, which makes it the ideal time to be aggressive. Double soft 13 through 18 against the dealer's weakest cards (4, 5, 6) because you get extra money down while the dealer is most likely to bust. Soft 18 is the trickiest hand in blackjack: it doubles against 2 through 6, stands against 7 and 8, and hits against 9, 10, and ace because 18 simply is not good enough against a strong dealer card.

Always split aces and eights. Two aces make a 12 or 2, both poor; split, and each ace starts a strong hand. Sixteen is the worst total in the game; two eights become two hands starting at 8. Never split tens: 20 is a near-certain winner and breaking it up throws away expected value. Never split fives: 10 is a doubling hand, not two weak hands.

Insurance and side bets

When the dealer shows an ace you will be offered insurance. Decline it. It is a bet that the dealer's hole card is a ten, and the payout of 2 to 1 is worse than the true odds, giving the house an edge above 7%. The same logic applies to nearly every side bet on the table.

How to actually learn it

  1. Start with the rules of thumb: split aces and eights, never split tens or fives, double 11, stand on hard 17+, hit soft 17 or lower.

FAQ

Common questions

Does basic strategy guarantee I win?

No. It minimizes the house edge to about 0.5% under these rules, which means you will still lose slowly over time. It is the best possible play, not a winning system.

Do I need a different chart for different rules?

Slightly. This chart is for six decks, dealer hits soft 17, double after split allowed. Single-deck games and dealer-stands-on-17 games change a handful of plays. House Edge Casino uses this rule set and shows this chart in the app.

Should I ever take insurance?

No. Insurance is a side bet on the dealer having a ten in the hole. In a six-deck game the house edge on it is over 7%. Decline it, even with a blackjack.
